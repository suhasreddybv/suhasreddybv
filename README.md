I build and validate signal-processing and forecasting methods for wearable physiological data, and I hold them to the standard a regulator would — published protocol first, every claim tied to a number in a committed file.

I'm a physician by training, now finishing an M.S. in Applied Cognition and Neuroscience at UT Dallas, where I work as a graduate research assistant in the LiNC Lab. At Myna Voice Labs I lead the clinical and regulatory work. The clinical background shows up here as the questions I ask of a method — what it does on the readings that matter, at a false-alarm rate someone would tolerate — rather than as the subject.

**Selected work**

- [`wrist-ppg-motion-robust-hr`](https://github.com/suhasreddybv/wrist-ppg-motion-robust-hr) — a replication of a published wrist-PPG heart-rate method under its own rules, with a per-activity table its authors didn't publish and a signal-quality index shown to be anti-predictive of error under motion.
- [`cgm-forecast-conformal`](https://github.com/suhasreddybv/cgm-forecast-conformal) — a glucose forecaster placing fifth in the published 2020 ranking under the challenge's rules, whose hypoglycaemia alarm detects 27% of onsets at two false alarms a day; conformal intervals shown to add an offset and nothing more at 30 minutes.
- [`neuron-models-from-scratch`](https://github.com/suhasreddybv/neuron-models-from-scratch) — Hodgkin–Huxley and integrate-and-fire from first principles, with convergence and f–I checks against closed forms.

**Currently** — reproducing a published wearable-sensing paper under its own protocol (`nir-glucose-reproduction`, in progress), and writing an FDA-validation memo on what the iCGM special controls would ask of a forecaster.
